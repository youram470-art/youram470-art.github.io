A short glossary for browser video work

2026-11-26 · working notes

Browser video tools borrow their vocabulary from film and their constraints from web browsers, which is why the same word often means two things in one conversation. These six definitions are the ones that cause the most confusion.

Prompt adherence
How much of the written description survives into the frame. High adherence with a long prompt usually means a busy, literal image; low adherence with a short prompt is often just a different picture than the one you asked for.
Frame budget
The number of frames a model can keep consistent before detail starts to drift. It is a property of the model and the length of the shot, not of your prompt.
Drift
The slow accumulation of small errors across a shot: background detail softening, fabric changing colour, a face losing its shape. Drift is why the last second of a long shot rarely matches the first.
Drift is the reason a frame budget is worth measuring in the first place: a shot that looks stable in the H3 Video player at second two can be visibly soft by second six.
Temporal coherence
Whether consecutive frames describe the same world. A shot can be coherent for two seconds and incoherent for six, and the number is worth knowing before you plan a cut.
Still-first workflow
Generating the composition as an image, then animating it. Cheaper to iterate and easier to review, because a still is a document other people can point at.
Attempt limit
A number chosen in advance for how many generations a shot gets. The limit is what turns a hobby into a process, and four is a reasonable default.

The terms matter less than the habit of using them consistently. A team that agrees on what drift means will argue about the right thing, which is usually whether the shot is worth another attempt.
