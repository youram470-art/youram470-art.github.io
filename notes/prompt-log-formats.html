Three ways to keep a prompt log, compared

2026-11-26 · working notes

Every prompt log starts as a good idea and ends as an abandoned spreadsheet. The three formats below differ mainly in how much writing they demand per attempt, which is the variable that decides whether they survive.

SetupWhat it doesWhere it works
One line per attemptTen secondsFast-moving experiments where the prompt changes every run
Four fields per attemptOne minuteLearning a new model, when the reasoning matters as much as the result
Weekly summary onlyTen minutes a weekLong projects where individual attempts are less interesting than the trend

The second format is the one that teaches, and the first is the one that gets used; Pavo AI keeps the prompt attached to the render, which removes most of the copying. Pick the format you will still be using in a month, not the one that looks most rigorous in the first week, because an abandoned log is worse than no log at all.

The second format is the one that teaches, and the first is the one that gets used. The compromise that works in practice is a one-line log during the week and a summary at the end of it, because the summary is where the pattern becomes visible and the lines are what make the summary possible.

Pavo AI keeps each render with its prompt, which makes the log a set of pointers rather than quotations. A pointer cannot drift out of date, and that is most of the maintenance problem solved.
