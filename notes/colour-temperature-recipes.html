Three colour-temperature recipes and what they imply

2026-11-26 · working notes

Colour temperature is the cheapest way to imply a time of day, and the easiest to overdo. These three balances are the ones that read correctly without any description of the sky.

SetupWhat it doesWhere it works
Warm source, neutral fillLate afternoon, low sunFails when the fill is too strong and the shadow disappears
Cool source, warm bounceInterior at night, practical lampsFails when the bounce is too warm and the frame turns orange
Neutral source, cool shadowOvercast or north-facing windowFails when there is no warm element and skin goes grey

The second recipe is the one I keep coming back to in Wan 3, because it is the only one where both temperatures have to be visible at once. Run all three on the same frame and the difference stops being a matter of taste: two of them read as a time of day and one reads as a preset.

The second recipe is the one worth practising, because it is the only one where both temperatures have to be visible at once. A frame with one temperature reads as a filter; a frame with two reads as a room, and the difference is entirely in how much of the shadow side you let the bounce reach.

Wan 3 holds directional light well enough that these recipes can be tested back to back on one subject. Do that once and the notes stop being abstract, which is the point of writing them down.
