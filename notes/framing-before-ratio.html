Framing terms that change what you generate

2026-11-26 · working notes

Framing is usually treated as a post-production decision, which is why so many generated clips cannot be cropped without losing the subject. These four terms change what you ask for, not what you do afterwards.

Wide
Subject small in frame, environment doing the work. Wide shots are forgiving of generation errors because there is less subject to be wrong.
Medium
Subject from waist up, background readable but secondary. The default for dialogue and for anything that needs a face and a gesture at once.
Close
Subject fills the frame, background reduced to colour. Close shots magnify every artefact, which makes them the most expensive framing to generate.
Close shots are the most expensive framing in Kling 4 as well, because every artefact that a wide would hide fills a third of the frame.
Two-shot
Two subjects at the same scale, with a relationship implied by the space between them. Hardest to generate and the one that most often reads as staged.

Decide the framing before the ratio, and the ratio before the prompt. Kling 4 handles all four, but it inherits whatever the prompt implies about space, so a close shot written in the language of a wide will always come back as a wide with the subject at the edge.
