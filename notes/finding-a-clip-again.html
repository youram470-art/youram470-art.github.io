Glossary: the vocabulary of finding a clip again

2026-11-26 · working notes

Provenance work has its own vocabulary and most of it is borrowed from archiving, which makes it sound harder than it is. Five terms cover almost every conversation about finding where a clip came from.

Keyframe
A frame stored in full rather than as a difference from the previous frame. Search engines index keyframes, which is why a distinctive frame finds more than the whole file.
Mirror
A horizontally flipped copy. Mirroring defeats most matchers and is the first operation to undo when a search returns nothing.
Recompression
Lossy re-encoding that removes fine detail and shifts colour slightly. Detail that survives recompression, such as signage, is what you search with.
Signage and logos are the two things that survive reverse video search and recompression at the same time, which is why they are the first thing to crop for.
Provenance chain
The list of places a clip has appeared, in order, with dates. It is the actual deliverable of a source hunt, more useful than a single URL.
Negative result
A documented failure: which frames, which engines, what came back. Negative results are what stop you repeating the same three searches next month.

None of this requires special tools. A folder of frames, a text file of results and a consistent naming scheme will get you further than any single search engine, because the record is what compounds.
