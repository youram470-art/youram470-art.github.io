Lighting notes compared: three setups, three results

2026-11-26 · working notes

Lighting descriptions fail in a specific way: they name a mood, the model returns even fill, and the frame goes flat. The three notes below are written as instructions instead, and each one has a predictable effect on shadow shape.

SetupWhat it doesWhere it works
Window light, left, hard edgeOne source, short distance, visible shadowPortrait and product, reads as daylight interior
Overcast, overhead, soft edgeNo visible source, shadows wrap under the subjectDocuments the subject, low drama, good for texture
Single warm source, low right, cool bounce leftTwo temperatures, shadow side stays readableInteriors at night, faces keep their shape

Both of the last two recipes are worth trying in Grok's image generator on the same subject, because the difference between them is the difference between a room and a filter. The test is to run the same three notes on one subject and compare the shadow edges rather than the colours, because colour is what the eye notices and shadow shape is what the frame is actually built from.

The middle row is the safe one and the last is the interesting one. Overcast light is hard to get wrong because there is no direction to contradict; a warm source with a cool bounce is easy to get wrong because the balance has to be deliberate, which is why it teaches more per attempt.

Write the note before the subject. A lighting description that survives a change of subject is worth keeping, and the three above are the ones that kept working across portraits, rooms and objects.
