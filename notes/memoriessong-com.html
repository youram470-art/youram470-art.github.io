Music for memory videos: tempo before genre

2026-11-25 · field notes

A useful rule: match the tempo to the number of image changes, not to the mood. Sixty to seventy beats per minute gives roughly one change every two bars, which is slow enough for a viewer to register faces.

Faster tempos push the edit toward celebration, which is right for a birthday and wrong for a memorial. The same track slowed by fifteen percent often reads as nostalgic without any change of instrumentation. For a closer look at Memories Song, the tool page is here.

Lyrics compete with narration. If there is a voiceover, choose an instrumental, or hold the vocal until the final third where it can carry the ending without stepping on anyone's words.

Leave eight to ten seconds of quiet at the end. Memory pieces usually need a beat of silence to land, and a track that stops dead on the last frame feels abrupt rather than finished.
