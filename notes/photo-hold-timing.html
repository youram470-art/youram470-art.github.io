Photo timing: four answers about holds and cuts

2026-11-26 · working notes

Every slideshow decision is a timing decision, and most of them get made by feel at the moment the music is already playing. These four answers are the ones that hold up when you check them afterwards.

How long should each photo stay?

Four to six seconds for most images, with a couple of longer rests. Under four seconds the eye never lands, and the slideshow becomes a flicker with a soundtrack.

Should cuts land on the beat?

Only at section changes. Beat-cutting everywhere is exhausting; cutting at the chorus is what makes the chorus feel like an arrival.

How many photos per minute?

About twelve. More than that and the transitions eat the images; fewer and the pauses start to look like buffering.

Twelve per minute is the number Memories Song defaults to for a reason: it is the fastest rate at which a face still registers.

What do I cut first when it drags?

Photos, not time. Removing two images is almost always better than shortening twenty holds, because the remaining images get room to breathe.

Memories Song makes the hold length adjustable, which is the setting most people leave alone. Change it before choosing the music and the arrangement stops fighting the picture.
